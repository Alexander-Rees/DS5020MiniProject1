## Data engineering (partner's decisions, kept unchanged by `build_matrix.py`)

| # | Decision | Reason | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | Load both sheets and stack them; canonicalise column-name variants (`InvoiceNo`, `UnitPrice`, `CustomerID`) | The brief warns names differ between versions, and the split needs both years | `reports/sheet_counts.csv`, `reports/data_profile.csv` |
| D2 | Cleaning rules in this order, each row counted under the first rule that removes it: cancellations (`C` invoices) -> quantity <= 0 -> price <= 0 -> non-product codes -> missing customer ID | Cancellations and returns are not purchases; a zero price is not a sale; postage, fees, samples and vouchers are not products; the matrix needs a customer per row | `reports/cleaning_log.csv` (19,494 / 3,457 / 2,750 / 4,666 / 234,370 removed; 802,634 kept) |
| D3 | Non-product codes = an explicit list (`POST`, `DOT`, `M`, `m`, `C2`, `D`, `S`, `BANK CHARGES`, `AMAZONFEE`, `B`, `ADJUST`, `ADJUST2`, `CRUK`, `TEST001`, `TEST002`, `PADS`) plus the `gift_0001_` voucher prefix. Codes with a trailing letter (`85123A`) stay as separate products. `SP1002` stays | An explicit list is auditable; variants are different colours or sizes that customers choose separately | `NON_PRODUCT_CODES` in `build_matrix.py`; test `test_cleaning_rules_apply_in_order_and_keep_variants` |
| D4 | Test period = last 2 calendar months: cutoff = max timestamp - `DateOffset(months=2)` = 2011-10-09 12:50:00. Training is strictly before it | The brief's split; a time split mimics "predict what they buy next" | `reports/split_log.csv`, `reports/matrix_report.json` |
| D5 | The test set keeps only purchases of products the customer never bought before the cutoff (45,644 repeat rows excluded) | A recommender should suggest something new; repeat purchases are trivially predictable and are excluded from recommendations anyway | `split_by_time`; test `test_split_is_chronological_and_test_has_only_new_pairs` |
| D6 | Purchase value = log(1 + total quantity), summed per customer and product **before** the log | Raw quantity is dominated by a few wholesale lines; 1/0 ignores repeat orders | `build_matrices`; test `test_quantities_are_summed_before_log_transform`; ML7 checks the choice |
| D7 | Keep products bought by >= 10 **training** customers (3,687 of 4,530); drop customers left with no products (1) | Products with a handful of buyers have unreliable factors; using only training data avoids leakage | `reports/matrix_report.json` |
| D8 | Sorted, stable row/column order; product name = most frequent training description (ties: alphabetical; none: the stock code) | Deterministic mappings; names come only from training data | `product_descriptions`; test `test_descriptions_use_training_mode_with_ties_and_fallback` |

## Completion decisions (2026-10-12)

| # | Decision | Reason | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | Main method: Truncated SVD on the sparse matrix (partner's ML1, kept) | Native on sparse data; zeros keep meaning "not bought"; gives customer and product factors in one fit | Notebook ML1 |
| C2 | Solver `algorithm="arpack"` instead of the default randomized solver | It gives the exact top-k (same errors as the partner's dense SVD to 4 decimals) at about the same cost (about 0.2 s at k = 100). Randomized gave 0.7279 instead of 0.7268. Decided from training reconstruction error only; PCA uses the same solver family | Notebook ML2 table "partner dense SVD vs sparse ARPACK fits" |
| C3 | k = 100 (partner's predeclared value, kept) | Training error still falls at k = 100 but each added component buys less; k = 100 is the largest swept k whose factors (7.33 MB) fit the 10 MB target (k = 200 needs 14.66 MB). Test hit rates were **not** used | `reports/svd_curve.csv`, `figures/svd_curve.png`; `reports/k_test_descriptive.csv` is descriptive only |
| C4 | The dense `np.linalg.svd(A.toarray())` from the partner's ML2 is historical only; the operational model never densifies A | The dense copy alone is 161 MB, which is what Sarah asked us to avoid | `model_analysis.py` |
| C5 | Reconstruction error by `||A||^2 - ||AV||^2` (V orthonormal), with no dense reconstruction | Exact and sparse-friendly | Test `test_sparse_svd_error_matches_dense_reconstruction` |
| C6 | Recommenders: product vectors = `normalize(components_.T)`; item neighbours = top-10 cosine excluding self; customer score = (A_row P) P^T, i.e. similarity to each past product weighted by its purchase value; every bought product excluded; ties to the lower index. Every top-10 list is exactly 10 wide; when fewer candidates remain it is padded with index -1 (score -inf), which consumers drop before looking products up | Follows ML3; deterministic; the weighting matches the matrix value; fixed-width arrays work for any catalogue size | Tests `test_recommend_similar_*`, `test_customer_scores_*`, `test_top_n_*`, `test_tiny_catalogue_pads_every_method_to_n` |
| C7 | Item neighbours computed in batches; only the 3,687 x 10 index and score tables are stored | Avoids keeping a 3,687 x 3,687 similarity matrix | `item_neighbors` |
| C8 | Baseline = catalogue products ranked by total training quantity; each customer's bought products removed and the list refilled to 10 | The brief's R3, applied to the same catalogue and history as the model | Test `test_best_sellers_rank_by_training_quantity_and_refill_after_seen` |
| C9 | Primary evaluation cohort = warm matrix customers with >= 1 held-out product in the catalogue (1,913). One hit per customer if any held-out product is in the top 10. Recall at 10 = mean over customers of hits / that customer's held-out catalogue products (denominator not capped at 10). Coverage is reported three ways: customers 82.9% (1,913 / 2,308), (customer, product) pairs 70.7% (`eligible_pair_coverage_pct`, 44,727 / 63,252) and distinct products 89.7% (`eligible_product_coverage_pct`, 2,555 / 2,849); the warm and all-test rates are reported alongside | One fixed denominator for every method; coverage must stay visible and each rate must say what it counts | `reports/results.json` -> `evaluation`; tests `test_heldout_targets_and_hit_rate_denominators`, `test_recall_denominator_is_not_capped_at_ten` |
| C10 | Uncertainty via a paired bootstrap (2,000 resamples, seed 42) and an exact McNemar test | The cohort is modest; the reader needs to know whether a gap is noise | `evaluation.comparisons` |
| C11 | Every fit runs in a fresh subprocess; RSS sampled every 1 ms with psutil (plus the `ru_maxrss` cross-check); time = median of 5 runs; BLAS/OpenMP threads = 1 | Fair, repeatable time and memory; earlier fits cannot inflate a later peak | `run_worker`, `environment` block in `results.json` |
| C12 | PCA comparison: `PCA(100, svd_solver="arpack", random_state=42)` on the same sparse log matrix, implicit centring, no scaling; factor size includes the mean vector | Same matrix, k and solver family; scaling would change the data being compared | `reports/comparison.csv` |
| C13 | Nightly computation stage = fit + normalise + top-10 for every product, timed as a kernel benchmark (median of 5 runs) on the prepared in-memory matrix; it excludes raw Excel cleaning, imports, loading, saving and deployment/network. Personalised all-customer scoring timed separately | Product pages are the nightly deliverable; customer lists are optional | `results.json` -> `nightly` |
| C14 | Customer map: first two oriented SVD customer components in polar form (log10 distance = size, angle = mix); k-means with k = 4, `n_init=20`, seed 42, on the standardised pair. This is 2-D clustering, not clustering on all 100 components. The map shows every customer (full range, no cropping); a supplementary figure plots the literal component 1 and 2 scores with the same labels | Component 1 is volume and very skewed (k-means on the raw pair isolates 12 huge accounts). Polar form separates "how much" from "what". k = 4 gives groups of 811 to 1,910 customers. Training data only | `figures/customer_map.png`, `figures/customer_map_components.png`, `reports/segments.csv`; `docs/segment_analysis.md` |
| C15 | Segment names = volume tier (segment median quantity against all customers' median) + theme of the 10 most distinctive products (mix lift, minimum max(10, 2% of members) buyers), used only if the theme's keywords match at least half of them, otherwise "mixed gifts" (the occasional group's distinctive products are parasols, tea glasses, teacups, a candle, a fan and a shopper: only 4 of 10 are tea-ware). A geography tag only if the non-UK share is >= 2x the overall share | Names must come from products and behaviour; 91% of customers are UK, so geographic names would be ungrounded | `segment_profiles.json` |
| C16 | ML7: refit k = 100 with count and 1/0 values; same scoring and cohort; **log stays the main model** | The value was fixed in DE4 before testing; choosing on the test set breaks R2. Binary's +1.7 points (interval -0.1 to +3.6) is a lead for a future validation window | `reports/sensitivity.csv` |
